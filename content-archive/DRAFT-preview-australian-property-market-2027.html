Australian Property Market 2027: What the Experts Predict, and What It Means for Sydney

If you have been to a barbecue in Sydney lately, you have probably heard the same question: what is going to happen to property prices in 2027? After five interest rate rises in one year, seven straight months of falling home values and a once-in-a-generation shake-up of investor tax rules, people are anxious, and fair enough. But anxiety makes a poor financial adviser. So we have pulled together what the Reserve Bank, the major research houses and the people who forecast this for a living are actually saying, and what it means for homeowners, buyers, investors and renters in Liverpool and South West Sydney.

The Short Version

  • The market is falling, not freefalling. National home values fell 1.2% in October 2026 and sit 6.7% below their March peak, according to Cotality. Sydney is more than 10% below its February peak.

  • Rates are the whole story. The RBA lifted the cash rate to 4.85% on Melbourne Cup Day, its fifth rise of 2026. Most bank economists do not expect a cut until the second half of 2027.

  • Forecasters see a soft start, then a slow turn. Almost nobody predicts a 2027 boom, and almost nobody predicts a crash on the scale of the doom headlines.

  • Circle 1 July 2027. That is when the Federal Budget's capital gains tax changes are due to start, alongside the wind-back of negative gearing on established homes.

  • South West Sydney is not Sydney's average. Affordable homes have fallen less than expensive ones, first home buyers are concentrated here, and the new airport has just opened next door.

How We Got Here

Twelve months ago, everyone was talking about rate cuts. The cash rate sat at 3.60% and the next move was assumed to be down. Instead, inflation proved stubborn and the Reserve Bank raised rates in February, March, May, September and again on 3 November 2026, taking the cash rate to 4.85%, its highest level in about 18 years.

The November decision was closer than the others. The board split six votes to three, with three members preferring to hold, which many market watchers read as a sign the hiking cycle is nearly over.

The effect on borrowers has been brutal. Cotality's head of research Gerard Burg estimates that four of this year's rises cut a median household's borrowing capacity by roughly $71,000. When buyers can borrow less, they pay less, and prices follow. At the end of October 2026, Cotality's figures showed:

  • National: down 1.2% for the month, the seventh fall in a row, and about $63,400 off the median dwelling value since March.

  • Sydney: down 1.6% for the month and 9.4% over the year, with a median dwelling value of about $1,178,959, now below where it was five years ago.

  • Brisbane and Perth: falling faster than Sydney after enormous five-year run-ups.

One detail matters a lot for our part of Sydney. Cotality's September figures showed the most expensive quarter of Sydney houses had fallen about 10.7% from the peak, while the most affordable quarter had fallen roughly half as far. Much of that affordable quarter is in Western and South West Sydney.

What the Forecasters Expect for 2027

Forecasts are educated guesses, and the experts disagree. Here are the main views, with the date each was made, because timing matters when rates have moved this much.

  • Australian Property Institute (survey of 384 experts, October 2026): a total fall of 8% to 11% from the March peak, meaning the decline is only about halfway done. Recovery is expected to start between July and September 2027.

  • Domain (June 2026): Sydney house prices down 3% to 7% in the year to June 2027, roughly $52,000 to $122,000, with units holding up better as buyers trade down.

  • CommBank (September 2026): a national peak-to-trough fall of about 9%, and around 13% for Sydney.

  • realestate.com.au and PropTrack (June 2026): the most optimistic view, with Sydney ending 2026 about 3% lower and then rising 4% in 2027. But it assumed rates had peaked at 4.35%. They have risen twice since, so treat it as the best case.

Put together, a rough consensus appears: more softness in the first half of 2027, a floor forming around mid-year, and a slow recovery in the second half, if inflation cooperates. That last condition is doing a lot of work.

The Five Forces That Will Decide 2027

1. Inflation and the RBA

The RBA's November forecasts have headline inflation at 3.7% in the year to June 2027, easing to 2.7% by December, with underlying inflation not back in the middle of the target band until mid-2028. The board says risks are "skewed to the upside." Westpac has pencilled in a first cut as early as August 2027, and ANZ and NAB also expect cuts in the second half. Cotality expects values to keep falling until rates ease. In short, the quarterly inflation numbers will move the market more than any auction result.

2. Jobs

The best protection against a crash is employment. People who keep their jobs generally keep their homes, even when repayments hurt. The RBA expects unemployment to drift from 4.7% to 4.9% over 2027, a slow grind rather than a collapse. If job losses come faster, forced sales would rise and the downturn would deepen.

3. The investor tax shake-up

The May 2026 Federal Budget announced the biggest change to property investment tax in decades. Negative gearing is being limited to new builds, so investors buying established homes lose it. From 1 July 2027, the 50% capital gains tax discount is due to be replaced by indexation plus a 30% minimum tax on real gains, with gains made before that date keeping the old treatment.

REA Group's economists expected these changes to shave "a couple of percentage points" off price growth in 2026 and 2027. Expect investors to favour new builds over older homes and units, and some long-term holders to weigh up when to sell. If you own an investment property, talk to your accountant early in 2027, not in June.

4. First home buyers and the $1.5 million cap

As investors step back, first home buyers have stepped up. More than 102,000 Australians have used the expanded 5% Deposit Scheme since October 2025, and about one in two first home buyers now use it. Sydney's price cap is $1.5 million, the highest in the country.

That creates a two-speed market, with far more buyers chasing homes under the cap than over it. InfoTrack data for the September 2026 quarter put Liverpool in the top 10 NSW suburbs for first home buyer activity, alongside Parramatta, Penrith and Blacktown. That demand helps explain why entry-level homes in South West Sydney have held up better than the headlines suggest. Read more in our guide to the 5% Deposit Scheme.

5. Supply and rents

Construction has been slow and costly for years, and higher rates are making developers even more cautious. Fewer completions in 2027 will put a floor under prices once confidence returns.

Rents are more nuanced. SQM Research shows Sydney's vacancy rate rose to 1.8% in September 2026, up from 1.3% a year earlier. House rents are still climbing, to about $1,159 a week, but unit rents fell 1.0% in a month to about $749. "Sydney is the market to watch," says SQM's Louis Christopher, who sees rental pressure "beginning to ease, starting with the unit market." Renters may finally get more choice in 2027, and landlords will need to price carefully.

What 2027 Could Mean for Liverpool and South West Sydney

National headlines treat Sydney as one market. It is not, and several things make 2027 look different from where we sit in Liverpool.

  • The airport is open. Western Sydney International welcomed its first passengers on 25 October 2026. Singapore Airlines starts daily flights on 23 November and Qantas is due in March 2027. New routes bring jobs, and jobs bring housing demand. We covered this in what the Western Sydney Airport means for property.

  • Affordability works in our favour. PRD put Liverpool's median house price at about $1.27 million and the median unit at about $599,000 in early 2026. Many homes across the region sit under the $1.5 million Deposit Scheme cap.

  • Mortgage stress is real. Digital Finance Analytics' June quarter 2026 data ranked the Liverpool postcode first in NSW by number of households in mortgage stress, before the last two rate rises. See what the rate rises mean for Western Sydney.

  • Suburbs will move differently. An established house in Casula or Moorebank, a new home in Leppington or Austral and an older unit in the Liverpool CBD may tell very different stories in 2027, especially once the tax changes start favouring new stock.

Key Dates to Watch in 2027

  • Early each month: Cotality's Home Value Index. The first flat or rising month will make headlines.

  • Late January and late April: quarterly inflation figures, the numbers the RBA watches most closely.

  • February: the first RBA decision of 2027.

  • 1 July: the new capital gains tax settings are due to begin.

  • Second half of the year: when most big four economists expect the first rate cut.

Frequently Asked Questions

Will house prices crash in Australia in 2027?

Most forecasters expect further falls in early 2027 rather than a crash, then stabilisation around mid-year. The Australian Property Institute survey expects a total fall of 8% to 11% from the March 2026 peak. A crash would most likely need a sharp rise in unemployment, which the RBA is not forecasting.

Will interest rates go down in 2027?

Possibly, but not soon. The cash rate is 4.85% after the November 2026 rise. Westpac, ANZ and NAB all expect the first cuts in the second half of 2027, depending mostly on inflation.

What will happen to Sydney property prices in 2027?

Forecasts range from further falls of several per cent (Domain, CommBank) to a 4% rise (PropTrack, made before the latest rate rises). The middle ground is a soft first half and a gradual recovery later in the year, with affordable areas holding up better than premium suburbs.

Is 2027 a good time to buy property in Sydney?

For buyers with secure jobs and solid pre-approval, a falling market brings more choice and better negotiating power. The risk is that prices dip further after you buy, so buy a home you plan to hold long term and can afford at a higher rate.

How are Liverpool property prices holding up?

Liverpool sits in the more affordable part of the Sydney market, which has fallen less than premium suburbs, and strong first home buyer demand and the new airport add support. Results vary by street and property type, so recent nearby sales are the best guide.

Bottom Line

2027 is shaping up as a year of transition, not catastrophe. The first half is likely to be tough, but the ingredients for a recovery, from slowing inflation and eventual rate cuts to limited supply and strong first home buyer demand, are already on the table. In South West Sydney, a new airport, relative affordability and a young population give the region more support than most. The people who do best in years like this are not the ones who guess the bottom. They know their own numbers, stay flexible and get good local advice.
