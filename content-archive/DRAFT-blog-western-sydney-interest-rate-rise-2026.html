On Tuesday 29 September 2026, the Reserve Bank lifted the cash rate to 4.60%, the highest it has been in 15 years. Two days later, new data showed Sydney home values had fallen another 1.4% in September alone. If you own, rent or are trying to buy in Western Sydney or South West Sydney, you're right in the middle of both stories. Below we break down the real numbers: what the rate rise costs per month, what's happening to prices, why our region feels it more than most, and what you can actually do about it.

The Short Version

  • Rates are up again. The RBA cash rate is now 4.60% after a 0.25 percentage point rise, the fourth increase of 2026. For homeowners, that pushes the average variable mortgage rate to around 6.5%, and many borrowers are paying 7% or more.

  • Repayments are up a lot more than one hike suggests. On a $600,000 loan, this year's four rises add about $364 a month, or roughly $4,368 a year.

  • Sydney prices are falling. Sydney dwelling values are down 1.4% in September, 4.9% over the quarter and 7.0% over the year, and now sit 8.6% below their February peak.

  • Western and South West Sydney carry the heaviest load. Seven of the 10 NSW postcodes with the most households in mortgage stress are in Western and South West Sydney, with Liverpool (2170) at number one by household count.

  • It's not all bad news. Homes are taking longer to sell and there's more stock to choose from, which gives prepared buyers real negotiating power for the first time in years.

What Actually Happened on 29 September

The RBA's board voted unanimously, nine to zero, to lift the cash rate from 4.35% to 4.60%. Its reasoning was simple: inflation hasn't come down fast enough. Headline inflation was running at 3.5% a year (July data) and the RBA's preferred trimmed mean measure at 3.6%, both above the 2–3% target band.

  • Four hikes in 2026: 100 basis points of tightening this year has more than wiped out the 75 basis points of cuts borrowers enjoyed in 2025.

  • Banks passed it on: the big four banks all passed the rise on in full to variable rate customers.

  • Your mortgage rate is not 4.6%: the cash rate is what banks pay to borrow overnight. Canstar estimates the average owner-occupier variable home loan rate rises to about 6.49% after this hike. Competitive lenders are offering around 6.25%, while investors and borrowers on older loans that haven't been repriced are often paying 7% or more.

  • More may be coming: some economists see another 0.25 point rise at the RBA's early November meeting (Melbourne Cup Day) as a real possibility, which would take the cash rate to 4.85%.

What the Rate Rise Costs You: The Real Numbers

One 0.25% rise sounds small. Four of them in a single year is not. Here's Canstar's estimate of the extra minimum monthly repayment for an owner-occupier paying principal and interest:

  • $500,000 loan: +$76 a month from this hike, +$303 a month across all of 2026

  • $600,000 loan: +$91 a month from this hike, +$364 a month across all of 2026

  • $750,000 loan: +$114 a month from this hike, +$454 a month across all of 2026

  • $1,000,000 loan: +$152 a month from this hike, +$606 a month across all of 2026

For a typical Western Sydney family with a $750,000 mortgage, that's about $5,450 a year in extra repayments compared with January, money that used to go on groceries, school costs, fuel or savings. (Canstar's figures assume 25 years remaining on the loan in February 2026, the RBA's average existing variable rate, and banks passing each rise on in full.)

Sydney Prices: Falling, But Not Evenly

Cotality's Home Value Index for September shows Sydney leading the national downturn. The headline numbers:

  • All Sydney dwellings: −1.4% for the month, −4.9% for the quarter, −7.0% for the year. Median value: $1,198,596.

  • Sydney houses: −1.6% for the month and −8.2% for the year. Median value: $1,466,060.

  • Sydney units: −1.0% for the month and −3.8% for the year. Median value: $867,597.

  • From the peak: Sydney values are 8.6% below their February 2026 high, a slightly deeper fall than at the same stage of the 2022–23 downturn.

  • Broad-based: 97% of capital city suburbs lost value over the three months to the end of September.

Why this matters for the west

The biggest falls are happening at the top end of the market. Cotality found upper-quartile Sydney house values are now 10.7% below their peak, while the gap to the lower quartile is 5.3 percentage points, meaning the most affordable quarter of Sydney houses has fallen roughly half as far. Much of Sydney's more affordable housing sits in the west and south-west, so the price story in suburbs like Liverpool, Campbelltown, Blacktown and Penrith is typically softer than the eye-catching citywide headlines suggest.

Why Western & South West Sydney Feels It More

Prices may be holding up comparatively well out west, but repayment pressure is a different story. Survey data from Digital Finance Analytics for the June quarter of 2026, measured before the September rise, put seven Western and South West Sydney postcodes in the NSW top 10 for households in mortgage stress:

  • Liverpool (2170): 14,705 households in stress, the most in NSW, up 12.7% on a year earlier

  • Campbelltown (2560): 12,946 households

  • Camden (2570): 11,089 households, up 28.4% in a single quarter

  • Riverstone (2765): 9,485 households

  • Mount Annan (2567): 8,458 households

  • Hoxton Park (2171): 7,002 households

  • Mount Druitt (2770): 6,498 households

Why here? Three reasons stand out:

  • Younger mortgages. The west is where many first home buyers and young families have bought in recent years, so a large share of loans are big, new and haven't had time to be paid down.

  • Less buffer. Established owners with years of savings and offset balances can absorb higher repayments. Newer buyers usually can't.

  • Cost of living bites harder. Longer commutes, larger households and higher fuel and grocery spending leave less room in the weekly budget.

Two Buyers, Two Very Different Stories

Recent ABC News reporting captured how differently this market lands depending on where you are in your property journey.

  • The new owner in Rooty Hill bought in April with help from family. Repayments now take around half of the household's regular income, and plans to start a family feel uncertain.

  • The 26-year-old owner in Parramatta spends about 30% of income on repayments, is comfortable staying put, and sees falling prices as a door opening for more young people to buy their first home.

Both are true at the same time. That's the reality of Western Sydney in late 2026: real pain for some recent buyers, and real opportunity for others who are ready to act.

The Upside: Buyers Have Bargaining Power Again

Softer demand has quietly changed the balance between buyers and sellers. Across Australia's capital cities in September:

  • Homes are taking longer to sell: a median of 39 days, up from 23 days a year ago.

  • Stock is building: total listings are 23.1% higher than a year ago, even though 9.2% fewer new listings came onto the market.

  • Fewer sales: Sydney's sales volumes over the past three months were 26.5% lower than a year earlier.

In plain English: more choice, less competition and more room to negotiate on price and terms. For a buyer with finance approved and a clear budget, conditions like these don't come around often.

What This Means for You

First home buyers

  • Your borrowing capacity has likely fallen since the start of the year. Get a fresh pre-approval before you house-hunt seriously.

  • Lower prices and less competition can partly offset higher rates, especially in the more affordable pockets of Western and South West Sydney.

  • Check government support. At the time of writing, NSW first home buyers pay no transfer duty on homes up to $800,000 (with concessions up to $1 million), and the federal 5% Deposit Scheme has a Sydney price cap of $1.5 million. Confirm the current rules with Revenue NSW and Housing Australia before you rely on them.

  • Budget for a buffer. Lenders test you at a higher rate than you'll pay for good reason, and another rise in November is possible.

Existing owners feeling the squeeze

  • You're not alone. Tens of thousands of households across the region are in the same position.

  • Talk to your lender early, before you miss a payment. Under the National Credit Code you can request a hardship arrangement, and your lender must respond in writing.

  • Selling is a big decision, not a first resort. But if you're weighing it up, find out what your home is realistically worth today rather than relying on last year's numbers.

Sellers

  • Price realistically from day one. With homes taking longer to sell, overpriced listings sit, and stale listings attract lower offers.

  • Presentation and marketing matter more in a buyer's market. You're competing with more stock than a year ago.

Investors and landlords

  • Sydney's vacancy rate has risen to 2.3%, and its gross rental yield sits at 3.4%, the lowest of the capital cities. Rents are still rising (Sydney house rents are up 4.9% over the year), just more slowly.

  • Holding costs, including interest, insurance, maintenance and strata, mean positive cash flow is rare. Run the numbers carefully before buying.

  • A quality tenant and a well-managed property matter more than ever when vacancies are rising.

Feeling the Pinch? Six Practical Steps to Take Now

  1. Call your lender and ask for a better rate. Many borrowers on older loans are paying well above what new customers get.

  2. Compare with a broker. Refinancing can save thousands a year, but check break costs, fees and whether you'll still pass a serviceability test.

  3. Use your offset account. Every dollar sitting in an offset reduces the interest you're charged.

  4. Rebuild your budget for 4.60%, and test it at 4.85%. Knowing your numbers ahead of November beats being surprised by them.

  5. Ask about hardship early. Options can include a temporary repayment reduction, a short pause, or extending your loan term.

  6. Get free, independent help. The National Debt Helpline (1800 007 007) offers free financial counselling, and the Australian Financial Complaints Authority (AFCA) can step in if you're unhappy with how your lender responds.

What to Watch Next

  • Late October: the September quarter inflation figures from the ABS, the key data the RBA will weigh before its next decision.

  • Early November (Melbourne Cup Day): the next RBA rate decision. Another rise to 4.85% is on the table.

  • Start of November: Cotality's October home value results, which will show whether the downturn is deepening or steadying.

  • Jobs data: Cotality notes a reasonably tight labour market is one of the main things limiting forced sales. Rising unemployment would change that picture.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the RBA cash rate now?

The cash rate is 4.60%, after the Reserve Bank lifted it by 0.25 percentage points on 29 September 2026. It's the highest level in 15 years and the fourth rise of 2026.

What interest rate will I actually pay on my home loan?

More than 4.6%. The cash rate is the RBA's benchmark, and banks add their own margin on top. Canstar estimates the average owner-occupier variable rate is about 6.49% after the September hike, with competitive rates around 6.25%. Investors and borrowers on older loans are often paying 7% or more, which is a good reason to ask your lender for a better deal.

How much will my repayments go up?

According to Canstar, this single rise adds about $91 a month to a $600,000 loan and $152 a month to a $1 million loan. Across all four 2026 rises, a $600,000 loan is about $364 a month more expensive than at the start of the year.

Are property prices falling in Western Sydney?

Sydney-wide values fell 1.4% in September and 7.0% over the year. The steepest falls are in the premium end of the market. More affordable homes, which make up much of Western and South West Sydney, have fallen by less. Individual suburbs vary, so local sales evidence matters most.

Is now a good time to buy in South West Sydney?

It depends on your finances, not the headlines. Buyers currently have more choice, longer selling times work in their favour, and competition is lower. But rates may rise again, so only buy with a solid pre-approval and a budget that still works at a higher rate.

Which Western Sydney areas have the most mortgage stress?

Digital Finance Analytics' June quarter 2026 data ranked Liverpool (2170) first in NSW by number of households in mortgage stress, followed in the state's top 10 by Campbelltown, Camden, Riverstone, Mount Annan, Hoxton Park and Mount Druitt.

What should I do if I can't afford my mortgage repayments?

Contact your lender as early as possible and ask about a hardship arrangement. You can also call the free National Debt Helpline on 1800 007 007. Acting early gives you far more options than waiting until payments are missed.

Bottom Line

The September rate rise hits Western and South West Sydney harder than most of the city, because this is where so many recent buyers and young families carry big new loans. At the same time, prices in the more affordable parts of the market are holding up better than the Sydney headlines suggest, and buyers have more bargaining power than they've had in years. Whether this market is a squeeze or an opportunity comes down to your own numbers. Know them, plan for one more rise, and get local advice before you make a move.
