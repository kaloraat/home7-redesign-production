Western Sydney Airport Opens: What It Means for Liverpool and South West Sydney Property

On Sunday 25 October 2026, Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport takes its first paying passengers. It's the first major new airport Sydney has had in more than a century, and it sits roughly 20 km west of Liverpool. If you live, rent, invest or are thinking about buying in Liverpool or South West Sydney, this is one of the biggest changes your area will ever see. Here's what the airport actually is, the facts most people don't know, and what it really means for local property prices, rents and buyers.

The Short Version

  • It's open. Passenger flights start 25 October 2026. Freight flights started in July.

  • It never closes. Unlike Sydney Airport at Mascot, Western Sydney International has no curfew, so planes can fly 24 hours a day.

  • It's a jobs machine. More than 12,800 jobs have been created during construction, about half going to local workers, with tens of thousands more planned in the surrounding Aerotropolis.

  • It's one piece of a bigger puzzle. The airport is part of a near $18 billion Western Sydney investment, including the new M12 Motorway and a metro line due in 2027.

  • Property impact is real but uneven. Jobs and transport lift demand, but aircraft noise, an already soft Sydney market and high interest rates all matter too. Location within the region makes a big difference.

Western Sydney Airport: Key Facts at a Glance

  • Official name: Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport, airport code WSI

  • Location: Badgerys Creek, in the Liverpool local government area

  • First passenger flight: Jetstar to the Gold Coast, 11am on Sunday 25 October 2026

  • Runway: a single 3.7 km runway, long enough for the biggest long-haul jets

  • Terminal: one integrated terminal for international and domestic flights

  • Capacity at opening: up to 10 million passengers a year

  • Long-term capacity: up to 82 million passengers a year by the 2060s, once fully built out

  • Curfew: none. It's a 24/7 airport

  • Timeline: about 15 years of planning, seven years of construction and a year of testing

Who Flies From Western Sydney Airport?

The launch line-up is small but growing:

  • Jetstar from 25 October 2026: up to 14 flights a week to Melbourne, 4 to the Gold Coast and 3 to Brisbane

  • Air New Zealand from 26 October 2026: flights to Auckland

  • Singapore Airlines from 23 November 2026: daily flights to Singapore, opening up connections to Asia, Europe and beyond

  • Qantas from 28 March 2027: 4 flights a week each to Brisbane and Melbourne

  • Qatar Airways from 15 December 2027: 3 flights a week to Doha on a Boeing 777, the first Middle East airline at the airport, with plans to go daily by the end of 2028. That means one-stop connections to Europe, the Middle East and Africa from Western Sydney.

For South West Sydney families, the practical win is simple. No more crossing the city to Mascot in peak traffic for a holiday flight or a trip home to see family overseas.

7 Interesting Facts Most People Don't Know

  1. It's named after a trailblazing aviator. Nancy-Bird Walton earned her pilot's licence as a teenager in the 1930s, flew outback medical runs in NSW and founded the Australian Women Pilots' Association.

  2. Sydney has waited over 100 years. Kingsford Smith Airport at Mascot started operating in 1920. Western Sydney International is the city's first major new airport since then.

  3. It was talked about for decades. Badgerys Creek was first floated as a second airport site back in the 1980s. It took more than 30 years to get from idea to first flight.

  4. The airport is technically in the Liverpool LGA. When people say "Western Sydney Airport", the land itself sits within Liverpool City Council's area.

  5. It's built to grow eightfold. Opening capacity is 10 million passengers a year, but the site is planned to handle up to 82 million when fully developed.

  6. Freight flew first. Cargo planes started using the airport in July 2026, months before passengers, which matters for the logistics jobs boom across Western Sydney.

  7. Local businesses have already won big. More than $500 million has flowed to over 360 Western Sydney businesses during construction, from tradies to caterers to security firms.

Kangaroos still hop past the new terminal, a reminder of how rural Badgerys Creek was until very recently.

Getting There: Roads and Rail

The M12 Motorway

The M12 links the M7 near Cecil Hills to the airport and on to The Northern Road. For Liverpool residents, it makes the airport a short drive away rather than a cross-city trek, and it improves east-west road access across the whole region.

Sydney Metro Western Sydney Airport line

  • Runs from St Marys to the new Bradfield City Centre, via Orchard Hills, Luddenham, Airport Business Park and the Airport Terminal

  • Currently planned to open in 2027, but the exact date has not been confirmed

  • Until it opens, buses and shuttles serve the airport

Road upgrades

Work is also underway on Elizabeth Drive, Fifteenth Avenue and Mamre Road. Fifteenth Avenue is especially important for Liverpool, Hoxton Park and Austral, as it's a key link between Liverpool and the airport area.

The Bigger Picture: The Aerotropolis and Bradfield City Centre

The airport is the anchor, but the real long-term story is what's being built around it.

  • Western Sydney Aerotropolis: a planned jobs and business precinct surrounding the airport, focused on advanced manufacturing, defence, aerospace, freight, logistics and research.

  • Bradfield City Centre: a brand-new city centre being built next to the airport. Its first building is already open and houses an Advanced Manufacturing Research Facility.

  • Private investment is flowing: the NSW Government says about $14 billion of private development applications are in the pipeline for the Aerotropolis, expected to create around 120,000 jobs.

Why does this matter for property? Because jobs drive housing demand. For decades, hundreds of thousands of Western Sydney residents have commuted east every day for work. As more good jobs arrive in the west, living close to them becomes more valuable.

What the Airport Means for Property Prices

The Sydney market is soft right now

The airport opens into a cooling market. Cotality data shows Sydney house values fell 1.6% in September 2026 and 5.6% over the September quarter. The Reserve Bank has lifted the cash rate four times this year, to 4.60%, and buyer confidence is low.

But affordable outer suburbs have held up better

Price falls in Sydney have been led by the premium end of the market. Earlier this year, Domain's Dr Nicola Powell noted that house prices in the outer south-west were up almost 13% over the year, even as the wider city started to fall. In most downturns, expensive areas fall first and affordable outer suburbs hold up better.

Infrastructure is a long game

  • Much is already priced in. The airport has been locked in for years, so buyers have been paying for its future value for a while. Don't expect prices to jump overnight because the first plane takes off.

  • The bigger gains come from jobs and transport. The metro line, the Aerotropolis and Bradfield City Centre will shape demand over the next 5 to 15 years.

  • Growth won't be even. Suburbs with good roads, schools, shops and a sensible commute to the new jobs usually do best.

Aircraft noise is the other side of the coin

A 24/7 airport means some homes will hear planes at night. The new flight paths came into effect in July 2026, and the government has said a "reciprocal runway" mode will be used at night where possible, sending planes over less populated areas. Being close to the airport is good for jobs and convenience, but sitting right under a flight path can reduce appeal for some buyers.

Tip: before you buy, check any address on the government's free WSI Aircraft Noise Overflight Tool at wsiflightpaths.gov.au.

Which Suburbs Are Closest to the Action?

Suburbs that are well placed to benefit from the airport, M12, metro and Aerotropolis jobs include:

  • Liverpool: the established CBD of South West Sydney, with a major hospital, university campuses, a train station and a short drive to the airport via the M12

  • Austral: a fast-growing land release area between Liverpool and the airport, along the Fifteenth Avenue corridor

  • Leppington: newer estates, a train station and quick road access to the airport precinct

  • Hoxton Park, Prestons, Edmondson Park and Bardia: family suburbs with good road links west

  • Bringelly, Kemps Creek and Luddenham: right next door to the airport, but with more flight path, zoning and development uncertainty

  • Penrith and St Marys: to the north, linked by the new metro line once it opens

What It Means for You

If you're buying

  • A soft market means more choice and more room to negotiate than you've had in years.

  • Focus on the basics: commute, schools, transport and flight paths. Don't pay a premium just for "airport" in the marketing.

  • Get your pre-approval done at today's higher rates, and budget for the hidden costs of buying in NSW.

If you're selling

  • The airport opening is a great story for your marketing, especially for buyers moving from other parts of Sydney.

  • In a slower market, price and presentation matter more than ever. Accurate pricing from local sales evidence wins.

  • Know your home's value before you list. A free market appraisal is a good starting point.

If you're an investor or landlord

  • Thousands of airport, freight, logistics and construction jobs mean more people looking to rent close to work.

  • Sydney's rental vacancy rate has been running tight, which supports rents in well-located suburbs.

  • Night shift and airline workers often want to live close to the airport, which can broaden your tenant pool.

Frequently Asked Questions

When does Western Sydney Airport open?

Western Sydney International Airport opens to passengers on Sunday 25 October 2026. The first commercial flight is Jetstar to the Gold Coast at 11am. Freight flights started in July 2026.

Where is Western Sydney Airport?

It's at Badgerys Creek, within the Liverpool local government area, roughly 20 km west of the Liverpool CBD and about 45 to 50 km west of the Sydney CBD.

Does Western Sydney Airport have a curfew?

No. Unlike Sydney Airport at Mascot, which has a night curfew, Western Sydney International can operate 24 hours a day.

Will the airport increase house prices in Liverpool?

Big infrastructure tends to support demand over time, mainly through new jobs and better transport. But much of the airport's value has been expected for years, and the Sydney market is currently soft, so any lift is likely to be gradual and will vary from suburb to suburb.

When will the metro to Western Sydney Airport open?

The Sydney Metro Western Sydney Airport line, from St Marys to Bradfield, is planned to open in 2027. An exact date hasn't been confirmed. Until then, buses and shuttles serve the airport.

How do I check if a home is under a flight path?

Use the federal government's WSI Aircraft Noise Overflight Tool at wsiflightpaths.gov.au. Enter an address to see expected flights and noise levels.
