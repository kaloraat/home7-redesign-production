Campbelltown's Queen Street is about to look very different. Sydney developer ALAND has approval for a $400 million mixed-use precinct on the old Brands on Sale site: 558 apartments in five towers of up to 15 storeys, a tree-lined "Eat Street", a public park and a community building, all a short walk from Campbelltown station. ALAND has now announced that Queen Square has officially opened its doors, with 70% of sales secured.

It lands in a city centre that is already changing, with a hospital redevelopment underway, Western Sydney University next to the station and a second five-tower project proposed just up the street. This guide covers what ALAND is building, where the project is up to, and what it could mean for buyers, investors and owners in Campbelltown.

ALAND's Campbelltown project at a glance

  • Developer: ALAND, one of Sydney's most active apartment developers.
  • Address: 22–32 Queen Street, Campbelltown NSW 2560 (marketed as Queen Square).
  • Value: about $400 million.
  • Homes: 558 apartments across five buildings, ranging from 12 to 15 storeys.
  • Retail and dining: more than 9,000 sqm of retail and commercial space, built around a tree-lined "Eat Street".
  • Public space: over 4,000 sqm of publicly accessible park and a two-level community building. More than 35% of the site is open space.
  • Site size: about 2.05 hectares.
  • Sales: ALAND has announced that Queen Square has officially opened its doors in Campbelltown with 70% of sales secured.
  • Approval: approved by the Regional Planning Panel in August 2023 after a 12-month assessment.
  • Design team: Sketch Design Group (architecture) and Sturt Noble (landscape).

What is being built on Queen Street?

The site sits at the northern end of Queen Street, the main street of the Campbelltown CBD. It is a large, flat, well-located block that has been empty for over a decade. Under ALAND's masterplan it becomes a new neighbourhood in its own right, rather than a single tower.

558 apartments in five towers

The five buildings range from 12 to 15 storeys. The published unit mix is roughly 97 one-bedroom, 415 two-bedroom and 46 three-bedroom apartments, which tells you who the project is aimed at: couples, small families, downsizers and investors, more than large family homes.

The design includes rooftop communal spaces, a podium-level plaza, more than 1,400 car spaces and bicycle parking. The masterplan started life as a much larger 780-apartment proposal in 2019 and was reduced to 558 after years of assessment and feedback, with more open space added.

"Eat Street" and a village-style centre

The focal point is a colourful, tree-lined dining street running through the middle of the site, with cafes, restaurants and shops. The retail plan has included an anchor supermarket, specialty retailers and a childcare centre. In total there is more than 9,000 sqm of retail and commercial floor space, plus jobs from the day the centre opens.

A park and a community building

The masterplan includes over 4,000 sqm of public open space with play areas, a bicycle loop and amphitheatre-style seating, next to a two-level community building with an auditorium and multipurpose rooms. Council has supported the project as a whole, and Campbelltown's mayor has said it helps deliver the goals of the council's Reimagining Campbelltown City Centre master plan.

From "Brands on Sale" to a new city gateway

Locals will remember the site. The Brands on Sale factory outlet centre opened in 2009 with around 80 shops, a food court and hundreds of car spaces. It went into receivership within about a year and left a large, empty shopping centre on one of the city's main approaches.

ALAND has been working on a replacement for years. A site hoarding designed by local students (more than 200 entries from four local schools, unveiled by the mayor in February 2023) was one of the first visible signs. The Regional Planning Panel then approved the project in August 2023. ALAND describes the goal as a new gateway to Campbelltown, with housing within walking distance of public transport and a place people actually want to spend time in.

Where is the project up to?

This is the question most people ask, so here is what we know. ALAND has announced on its Facebook page that Queen Square has officially opened its doors in Campbelltown, with 70% of sales already secured. That is a strong sign of buyer demand for new apartments near the station, and it means the project has moved well beyond the planning stage.

What is not yet public is a firm construction and completion program. ALAND's early materials targeted construction in 2023 and completion in 2025, and those dates have moved, as they often do with large apartment projects. Sales listings describe the apartments as offered off the plan.

Large precincts like this are usually built in stages over several years, so any completion date should be treated as a target, not a promise. If you are thinking of buying off the plan, ask the developer for the current construction program in writing and read the contract carefully. What do the apartments cost?

Advertised off-the-plan pricing has started from about:

  • 1-bedroom, 1-bath: from around $585,000
  • 2-bedroom, 2-bath: from around $695,000
  • 2-bedroom plus study: from around $735,000
  • 3-bedroom, 2-bath: from around $799,000

These are advertised starting prices from listing sites and can change. Confirm current pricing with the developer.

For comparison, established Campbelltown units are often cheaper than new off-the-plan stock, and houses in the area sit well above these figures. That is why new apartments close to the station attract first-home buyers and investors who want lower entry prices than a house.

Why Campbelltown, and why now?

Queen Square is not happening in isolation. Campbelltown is being positioned as a major regional city for south-west Sydney, and the pieces are falling into place.

1. A hospital that is growing

Campbelltown Hospital is being redeveloped, with a new multi-storey clinical services building, an expanded emergency department and more surgical theatres. Construction is underway, with completion targeted for around 2028–29. The hospital serves a catchment of roughly 500,000 people across Campbelltown, Camden and Wollondilly. Hospitals mean jobs, and jobs mean people who want to live close by.

2. Western Sydney University next to the station

The Western Sydney University Campbelltown campus sits beside the railway station and anchors a growing education and health precinct. Health sciences, social work and business students bring daily activity and rental demand to the centre, with placement partnerships with the local health district.

3. Transport and higher density around the station

Campbelltown is on the T8 Airport & South line, and station upgrades are underway. Planning rules for the transport-oriented development (TOD) precincts around Campbelltown, Leumeah and Minto encourage more homes close to stations. A future Metro South West extension is in planning, which would improve connections further.

4. A council with a plan

Campbelltown City Council's city centre strategy names Queen Street as a priority for mixed-use redevelopment, with building heights up to around 15 storeys in the core. The council has also been investing in public spaces and creative uses for the street, such as converting the old aviary at 261 Queen Street into an arts hub in 2024.

ALAND is not the only project on Queen Street

A second major proposal sits at 34 Queen Street, the site of Officeworks, the MCAS Superstore and a medical centre. H&R Properties has proposed a roughly $200 million, 395-apartment scheme of five towers up to 15 storeys, with about 2,000 sqm of retail and commercial space. It received gateway approval from council and went on public exhibition in February 2025.

Put the two together and around $600 million of new development is planned along a single street, adding close to 950 apartments if both proceed. Queen Street is set to shift from a tired retail strip to a genuine mixed-use spine.

What this could mean for buyers

  • First-home buyers: new apartments near the station offer lower entry prices than houses, and NSW first-home concessions and the federal 5% deposit scheme may apply. Check current caps and eligibility.
  • Investors: a big new residential and retail precinct increases demand for rentals nearby, but it also adds supply. Ask how many apartments are coming to market and when.
  • Downsizers: lifts, a walkable centre, dining and a park are a strong package for owners leaving a large family home.
  • Off-the-plan buyers: completion dates, valuation risk at settlement and sunset clauses all matter. Get independent advice before you sign.

What this could mean for owners in Campbelltown

Major projects tend to lift the whole area's profile: better shops, a stronger city centre, and more people spending locally. Owners of houses and units within walking distance of Queen Street and the station are the most likely to feel it, although nothing is guaranteed and the effect takes years, not months.
