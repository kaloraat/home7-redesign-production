Austral, in Sydney's South West Growth Area, has changed fast. Land that was once market gardens and paddocks is being rezoned and subdivided, new estates are going up around it, and Western Sydney International Airport is a short drive away. Not surprisingly, acreage here has caught the attention of investors, developers and families looking for space.

This guide explains why Austral acreage attracts long-term buyers, what the risks are, and what to check before you buy. Land can be a strong long-term investment, but it is not a guaranteed one, and the details of each block matter a great deal.

Why investors are interested in Austral acreage

  • Subdivision potential. Parts of Austral have been rezoned for residential development under the South West Growth Area plans. Where zoning and approvals allow, one large block can become many residential lots.
  • Sydney's growth to the south west. Population growth keeps pushing new housing toward the Liverpool and Camden areas, and Austral sits in the middle of that corridor.
  • Infrastructure. Western Sydney International Airport, motorway links and the rail station at nearby Leppington all improve access. Better access usually supports land values over time.
  • Lifestyle. Some buyers simply want room: a big backyard, space for a business or a hobby farm, and privacy you can't get on a standard suburban lot.

The risks to weigh up

Land behaves differently from a house or unit, and these are the points that catch buyers out:

  • Timing is uncertain. Rezoning, servicing and subdivision approvals can take many years, and some precincts move much more slowly than others.
  • Not every block can be subdivided. Zoning, flood planning, biodiversity, road reservations and easements can all limit what a particular block can become.
  • Holding costs add up. Land tax, council rates and loan interest continue while you wait, and land usually earns little or no rent.
  • Development costs. Subdividing involves infrastructure contributions, civil works, consultants and approvals, which can be a large share of the end value.
  • Prices can fall. Land values move with interest rates and the wider market, just like any other property.

What to check before you buy

  1. The planning certificate. Get the section 10.7 certificate from Liverpool City Council and check the zoning and any constraints.
  2. The precinct plans. Find out where the block sits in the growth area plans and how far that precinct has progressed.
  3. Services. Ask whether water, sewer and power are available or planned, and when.
  4. Professional advice. A town planner, solicitor and accountant can tell you what is realistic for the block and how it fits your finances.
  5. Recent sales. Compare similar blocks nearby, not headline prices from other suburbs.

Who Austral acreage suits

Austral acreage tends to suit buyers who can hold for the long term, have room in their budget for holding costs, and are comfortable with the uncertainty of planning timelines. Developers with experience in subdivision, families who want space now, and patient investors are the most common buyers.

If you need rental income straight away, or might need to sell within a few years, an established house or a vacant lot in a finished estate may be a better fit.
