How much do real estate agents charge to sell in Liverpool and Campbelltown?

Most agents charge a commission, which is a percentage of the final sale price, paid at settlement. Based on agent comparison data for 2026:

  • Across NSW, the average commission is around 1.85% including GST, with most agents somewhere between about 1.1% and 2.8%.
  • In Liverpool, reported averages sit at roughly 1.9% to 2%.
  • Across Sydney, a typical range is about 1.8% to 2.5%.

Campbelltown sits in the same South West Sydney market, so expect rates in a similar range. Treat all of these as a guide only. They come from comparison websites, and your own quote will depend on the property, the price range and how much work the sale involves.

To put that in dollars, on a $1,000,000 sale:

  • 1.8% commission is $18,000
  • 2.0% commission is $20,000
  • 2.5% commission is $25,000

Always ask whether a quoted percentage includes GST. A quote of "2% plus GST" is really 2.2%, which is another $2,000 on a million dollar sale.

Costs on top of commission

Commission is usually not the whole bill. Before you compare agents, ask each one for a written, itemised list of everything you will pay. Common extras include:

  • Marketing: professional photos, floor plans, listings on realestate.com.au and Domain, signboards and sometimes video. This is often charged separately and can be paid upfront even if the property does not sell.
  • Premium portal listings: upgrades that put your home higher in search results. Useful in some campaigns, unnecessary in others.
  • Auction costs: if you sell by auction, there may be a separate auctioneer fee.
  • Styling: furniture hire for a vacant home, usually quoted by a separate stylist.
  • Withdrawal or admin fees: some agreements charge you if you take the property off the market. Read this clause carefully.

You will also pay a conveyancer or solicitor to handle the contract and settlement. That cost is separate from the agent and worth getting a quote for early.

Property management fees in South West Sydney

If you own an investment property, the agent's fee is ongoing rather than a one-off. Typical Sydney fees in 2026 are:

  • Management fee: roughly 5% to 8% of the rent collected, plus GST at some agencies.
  • Letting fee: usually 1 to 2 weeks' rent each time a new tenant is found.

On a house renting for $650 a week, a 6% management fee works out to around $2,030 a year before any extras. The extras are where costs quietly add up, so ask about each of these:

  • Lease renewal fees
  • Routine inspection fees
  • Monthly admin or statement fees
  • End of financial year statement fees
  • Fees for attending the NSW Civil and Administrative Tribunal
  • Advertising fees when the property is vacant

A low headline percentage with five extra fees can end up costing more than a slightly higher percentage that covers everything.

Cheap agent or affordable agent? They are not the same thing

The cheapest commission is not always the cheapest result. The number that matters is what you have left after the sale, not the size of the agent's fee.

Here is a simple example. Agent A charges 1.5% and sells your home for $980,000. Agent B charges 2% and, through stronger negotiation, sells it for $1,010,000. Agent A's fee is $14,700 and you keep $965,300. Agent B's fee is $20,200 and you keep $989,800. The "more expensive" agent left you nearly $25,000 better off.

So an affordable agent is one whose fees are fair and clear and who can show you real results. Signs of good value:

  • Fees are written down before you sign, with no vague "costs to be confirmed".
  • The price estimate is based on actual recent sales near you, not a number designed to win your listing.
  • They have recently sold or leased homes in your suburb and can show you which ones.
  • You will deal with the same person throughout, and they answer when you call.
  • Google reviews mention communication and honesty, not just a quick sale.

Signs of a cheap agent who may cost you later: a very low fee paired with a very high price promise, pressure to sign the same day, or reluctance to put marketing costs in writing.

Can you negotiate real estate agent fees?

Yes. In NSW there is no fixed commission, so everything is open to discussion. A few approaches that work well:

  • Get two or three quotes from agents active in your suburb and compare them line by line.
  • Ask for a tiered commission, for example a standard rate up to a target price and a higher rate on anything above it. It rewards the agent for getting you more.
  • Ask what is included. Some agents include photos and portal listings in their commission. Others charge for everything separately.
  • Ask about fixed fees if your property is straightforward and you prefer certainty.
  • Negotiate marketing timing, such as paying at settlement rather than upfront.

Your rights before you sign an agency agreement

In NSW, the agency agreement is the contract between you and the agent. It must set out the commission and the expenses you will be charged, so read it closely and do not sign anything that leaves fees blank.

For residential sales there is also a cooling-off period. You can cancel the agreement in writing until 5pm on the next business day or Saturday after you sign. NSW Fair Trading publishes a plain-English guide to agency agreements, and your agent should give you a copy.

Do I pay the agent if my property does not sell?

Commission is normally paid only when the sale settles. Marketing costs and some admin fees may still be payable if it does not sell, depending on your agreement, so check those clauses before you sign.

Are property management fees tax deductible?

Property management fees on an investment property are generally a deductible expense. Check your own situation with your accountant.

Is a fixed-fee agent cheaper than a commission agent?

Sometimes. A fixed fee gives you certainty, but it can also mean less incentive for the agent to push for a higher price. A tiered commission is a good middle ground for many sellers.
