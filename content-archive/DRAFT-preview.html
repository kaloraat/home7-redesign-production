Real Estate Liverpool (preview of draft, not live)

Liverpool is changing faster than almost anywhere in Sydney. A new international airport opens on its doorstep in October 2026. The population is forecast to jump by more than 50% by 2041. The CBD is being rebuilt around a university, a hospital precinct and a new transport spine.

This guide gives you the facts, the numbers and the local knowledge behind real estate in Liverpool NSW, whether you're a first-home buyer, an investor, an upsizer or an owner thinking about selling.

Last updated: September 2026. We refresh this page as the market and major projects move.

Liverpool real estate at a glance

  • Houses: median price roughly $1.0–1.1 million (2170), with double-digit growth over the past year.
  • Units: mostly under $600,000, which is why Liverpool is one of NSW's busiest first-home-buyer postcodes.
  • Rents: around $650 a week for houses and $520–$540 for units.
  • Yields: roughly 3% for houses, 5.5–5.8% for units.
  • Distance: about 27 km south-west of the Sydney CBD, on the train line and the M5/M7 motorways.

Figures are approximate and come from public sales and rental data (NSW Valuer General and property data providers). Medians move every month, so ask us for the number that matters: your property's.

Why everyone is watching Liverpool

1. The airport is real, and it's days away

  • Western Sydney International (Nancy-Bird Walton) Airport opens to passengers on 25 October 2026, with freight from July 2026.
  • It's a $5.3 billion project. The airport company's head office is in Liverpool's city centre.
  • The wider Aerotropolis is expected to support around 200,000 jobs across the Western Parkland City over time.

2. Liverpool gets its own airport link

  • The Sydney Metro Western Sydney Airport line (St Marys to the airport) is due in 2027, but it doesn't stop at Liverpool.
  • Liverpool's connection is the Fifteenth Avenue Smart Transit (FAST) corridor: a dedicated rapid-transit route with over $1 billion committed. Major works are expected from around 2027.
  • Better access to the airport and Bradfield is a big reason investors are watching Liverpool's fringe suburbs.

3. A city that is growing up

  • Liverpool LGA's population is around 285,000 today and forecast to reach about 440,000 by 2041.
  • Planners expect roughly 2,700 new dwellings a year on average, reaching about 131,000 homes by 2041.
  • Higher densities are focused on the CBD and areas with good transport.

The Liverpool CBD is being rebuilt

  • Liverpool Civic Place: a major mixed-use precinct. The University of Wollongong is moving in, with further stages targeted for around 2028.
  • Health: Liverpool Hospital is one of the biggest employers in South West Sydney, and it keeps growing. Healthcare workers need homes nearby.
  • Education: university and TAFE campuses bring students, staff and rental demand.
  • Public space: upgrades to George and Moore Streets and the 28-hectare Woodward Park project are lifting how the city feels.

Why it matters: jobs, study and healthcare in one place are what create lasting property demand, not just a short-term buzz.

Council strategy and the rules that shape prices

  • Council's housing strategy: Liverpool City Council directs the highest densities to the CBD and areas with good transport, and keeps many established suburbs lower-density.
  • State planning reforms: NSW's low and mid-rise reforms open up more housing types near stations and centres. That can lift land value on well-located blocks.
  • Granny flats: state rules make secondary dwellings easier to approve, which changes what a large block is worth.
  • Foreign buyers: NSW charges an extra surcharge purchaser duty (9% at last check) and restrictions currently apply to foreign buyers of established homes. Rules change, so get advice before you buy from overseas.
  • What it means for you: two identical houses on different streets can have very different potential. Zoning is the hidden variable.

Liverpool: past, present and future

  • 1810: Liverpool is founded by Governor Macquarie, making it one of Australia's oldest towns.
  • Today: a regional city with a hospital, university presence, Westfield and a busy rail hub.
  • July and October 2026: Western Sydney Airport opens for freight, then passengers.
  • 2027: the Metro line to the airport is due, and major FAST corridor works are expected to begin.
  • Around 2028: further stages of Liverpool Civic Place are targeted.
  • 2041: population forecast of around 440,000.

How the property landscape is changing

High-rise apartments in the CBD

  • The CBD has the most apartment growth in the region.
  • Older units can still be found for under $500,000. New-build prices are higher.
  • Investors see stronger yields on units, but supply matters. Ask us how much new stock is coming before you buy.

Townhouses, terraces and low-rise

  • NSW's low and mid-rise housing reforms allow more terraces, townhouses, manor houses and low-rise apartments near stations and town centres.
  • Rules vary by street and zone. Check the council's mapping before you plan anything.

Bigger blocks and granny flats

  • A secondary dwelling ("granny flat") can be up to 60 square metres and must sit on the same lot as a main house.
  • Blocks of around 450 sqm or more are typically the target, so confirm your lot with a certifier.
  • A granny flat can add rental income, or space for family. Many Liverpool owners now buy with this in mind.

The Liverpool buyer profile

  • First-home buyers: Liverpool (2170) has repeatedly ranked at or near the top of NSW for first-home-buyer sales.
  • NSW first-home concessions: stamp duty is waived on eligible homes up to $800,000, with a sliding concession up to $1 million.
  • Federal 5% deposit scheme: lets eligible first-home buyers buy with a much smaller deposit. Check current caps and eligibility.
  • Investors: attracted by yields, jobs and infrastructure.
  • Diverse community: Liverpool is one of Sydney's most multicultural cities, with strong Vietnamese, Arabic, Indian, Nepali and Pacific communities, plus many international students. That shapes demand for everything from apartments to multi-generational family homes.

Where prices are heading (an honest view)

  • The bull case: the airport, Aerotropolis jobs, population growth and better transport support long-term demand.
  • The caution: Liverpool's strong run means the easy gains may be behind us. Vacancy has eased in some investor-heavy pockets since the very tight levels of 2025.
  • The takeaway: the right property in the right pocket matters more than the postcode alone.

How to find the "hidden" pockets

  • Look for suburbs within a short trip of a station or the future FAST corridor.
  • Look for older homes on larger blocks. They give you options to renovate, add a granny flat or redevelop.
  • Compare price per square metre of land, not just the headline price.
  • Check what's being approved next door. New supply can help or hurt.
